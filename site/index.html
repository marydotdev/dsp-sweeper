Helpers are sorted into three types automatically

Sweeper

Has no route of their own and helps other drivers.

Rescue

Has their own route and also helped another driver with 10+ stops.

Pickup

Took only 1–2 stops in total, usually a package marked missing. Never counted as a rescue.

Got one wrong? Change it from the dropdown next to the helper's name. The change applies to that day only.

How it works

Set up in about a minute.

  1. 1

    Install the extension

    Add DSP Sweeper to Chrome. There's no account to create and nothing to configure.

  2. 2

    Open the Cortex routes page

    Sign in to Cortex and open the routes page, /operations/execution/dv/routes. The toolbar appears at the bottom-right of the screen.

  3. 3

    Confirm your sweepers

    The + button shows a count when helpers are detected. Confirm each one and their pin takes on a color.

Using the toolbar

Colored dots
Click a dot to open that sweeper's detail panel: current route and today's history.
Changing a color
In the detail panel, click the dot beside the sweeper's name, then pick a swatch.
Clock icon
Opens the history page in a new tab.
Reset icon
Clears today's sweeper list. History isn't affected. The list also resets itself at midnight, your computer's local time.

Adding past days to history

Today is saved while the routes page is open. For earlier days there are two ways, and both save the same data.

Manually

Use Cortex's own date picker to open an earlier day and wait for the routes to load. The day appears on the history page within a second or two. Switch back to today when you're done.

Automatically

On the history page, choose a range and click Load automatically…. The extension steps your open Cortex tab through each missing day, about 10 seconds a day, then returns it to today. It only runs when you start it, and you can stop it at any time.

Before you use the automatic option: it changes dates in your Cortex tab for you, using your sign-in. Automated use of your account may be limited by Amazon's terms, so check that it fits your DSP's rules. If in doubt, use the manual way.

Privacy & permissions

Your route data stays in your browser.

Nothing is sent anywhere

Sweeper lists and history are stored in Chrome on your computer. The extension has no server and makes no network requests of its own.

It only reads what Cortex shows you

DSP Sweeper never calls Cortex's API. It saves the route data Cortex has already loaded in your tab, and it doesn't change anything in Cortex.

Three permissions

  • storage Keeps your sweepers across page refreshes
  • unlimitedStorage Holds months of daily history
  • activeTab Reads the current tab's address when you open the summary panel

It runs only on logistics.amazon.com, and the toolbar only appears on the routes page.

Questions

Good to know.

Is DSP Sweeper made by Amazon?

No. It's an independent tool and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Amazon. You need your own Cortex access to use it.

Does it change anything in Cortex?

No. It's a visual layer on top of the routes page: it recolors pins on your screen and adds a toolbar. Routes, assignments, and stops are untouched.

The toolbar isn't showing. What should I check?

Make sure you're on the routes page (/operations/execution/dv/routes), then reload the tab. If you hid the overlay, click the broom icon in Chrome's toolbar and choose Show overlay.

What happens to my sweeper list overnight?

Today's list clears at midnight, your computer's local time, even in a tab left open. History is kept.

How far back can history go?

As far back as Cortex still has route data for.

Does everyone on my team see the same sweepers?

No. Data is stored per browser, so each dispatcher confirms sweepers on their own computer.

Which browsers does it work in?

Google Chrome on a desktop or laptop.
